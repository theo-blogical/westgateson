**Scripture**: **Romans 3:25–26**

### I. Opening: Confession and cultural desensitization

1. **Personal confession: obsession with mob movies**  
   - Ruth admits she is “obsessed” with mob movies, particularly *Casino* and *Goodfellas*, with *The Godfather* as her favorite.  
   - She even had *The Godfather Waltz* played at her wedding, “double‑approved” by her mother and fiancé.  
   - These movies are full of crime, family drama, violence, and blood, yet she can watch them repeatedly without flinching.

2. **Modern numbness to violence and blood**  
   - Most people watch horror or slasher movies (*Friday the 13th*, *Scream*, *Texas Chainsaw Massacre*, etc.) and are emotionally detached.  
   - On‑screen blood is treated as **Hollywood drama** and **entertainment**, not as a real sign of life‑loss.  
   - As a result, we have become **desensitized** to violence and blood; it’s just visual spectacle.

3. **Disconnect between the cross and our screens**  
   - Ruth points out the tension:  
     - We watch blood and violence on our screens for **entertainment**.  
     - At the same time, we see a **cross** hanging in our churches or homes.  
   - The cross is not just a nice image; it depicts actual, brutal execution.  
   - There is a **disconnect** between how we experience entertainment‑blood and how we understand the blood shed on the cross.

### II. The biblical meaning of blood = life

1. **Leviticus: life is in the blood**  
   - Ruth quotes **Leviticus 17:11** to ground the sermon:  
     - “For the life of the flesh is in the blood… and I have given it to you on the altar to make atonement for your souls.”  
     - [Bible Gateway – Leviticus 17:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+17%3A11&version=NIV)  
   - Scripture insists that **blood sacrifice is central**, not optional, to understanding salvation.  
   - The Bible is not obsessed with gore; it is using blood to reveal **how life works** and how God restores life.

2. **Biological explanation of blood’s role**  
   - Ruth explains blood in physical, biological terms to help us feel the weight of “life in the blood”:  
     - **Oxygen carrier**: every breath you take sends red blood cells carrying oxygen to every organ and cell.  
       - If blood stops flowing to the brain, brain cells begin to die.  
       - Without blood, we **suffocate**.  
     - **Nutrient carrier**: after a meal, nutrients are absorbed and transported by blood.  
       - Without blood, we **starve**.  
     - **Cleanser and healer**: in severe blood loss, doctors do blood transfusions.  
       - New, clean blood is introduced to restore and sustain life.  

3. **Literal connection to the spiritual**  
   - Ruth stresses that when the Bible says **“life is in the blood,”** it is not just poetic language.  
   - It is saying that life, sustenance, and cleansing all depend on blood.  
   - Blood keeps every part of our bodies alive, clean, and whole.  
   - That truth is then carried over into the **spiritual** realm: what blood does for our bodies, Jesus’ blood does for our souls.

### III. Sin breaks our relationship with God

1. **Sin attacks our relationship, not just a rule**  
   - Just as blood is necessary for physical life, **sin drains spiritual life**.  
   - Sin breaks our relationship with God and damages us from the inside out.  
   - Ruth emphasizes that **relationship**, not just rule‑breaking, is at stake.

2. **Romans 6:23 – the wages of sin is death**  
   - Ruth quotes **Romans 6:23**:  
     - “For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”  
     - [Bible Gateway – Romans 6:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+6%3A23&version=NIV)  
   - The word “wages” is important:  
     - It is not just a punishment God “inflicts” arbitrarily.  
     - It is the **natural result** of sin, like a wage that is earned.  
   - Sin drains us of life; it is relational rupture, not just a legal penalty.

3. **Sin and relational breakdown**  
   - Ruth compares it to **human relationships**.  
     - When there is a rift or tension, people often:
       - Deny reality.  
       - Shift blame.  
       - Attack.  
     - We sense that the relationship is not right, but the cost of healing must be paid by someone.  
   - In that process, someone has to **absorb the cost** and reintroduce life, like a blood transfusion.  
   - This pattern is mirrored in our relationship with God: sin breaks the relationship, and **someone has to pay**.

4. **Genesis 3 – the original rupture**  
   - Ruth goes back to **Genesis 3**, where Adam and Eve sin.  
   - God calls out, “Where are you?”  
     - This is not because God lacks information.  
     - It is an **invitation** to honesty, confession, and relationship.  
   - Adam blames Eve; Eve blames the serpent.  
     - They deflect, distance, and hide.  
   - The result is that they are not just breaking a rule; they are fracturing their relationship with God and with each other.  
   - Since then, humanity has been hiding from God rather than drawing near.

5. **Sin today: autonomy over trust**  
   - Ruth defines sin as:
     - Choosing **autonomy instead of trust** in God.  
     - Turning **inward instead of outward in love**.  
   - We hide behind:
     - **Competence** and **busyness**.  
     - **Social media** and **perfectionism**.  
     - **Good routines** and **distractions** that become destructive.  
   - She shares that she can hide behind her busy calendar or her “good ministry work” instead of being honest about her need for God.

6. **The core tension: How can broken people draw near to a holy God?**  
   - Ruth names the central question of Scripture:  
     - How can **broken people** come near a **holy God**?  
   - The answer is not, “Clean yourselves up first.”  
   - It is, “God has a way of bearing the cost of reconciliation Himself.”

### IV. The Old Testament sacrificial system

1. **Grace first, then system**  
   - God **rescued** Israel from slavery in Egypt before giving them the sacrificial system.  
   - The sacrificial system was not the way Israel **earned** God’s love.  
   - It was the way Israel **came near** His presence after already being His people.

2. **The sacrificial system as a mirror**  
   - Ruth says the whole system was a **mirror**:
     - It reflected the **human condition**.  
     - It revealed the **seriousness of sin**.  
     - It showed that **life must be exchanged** for life.  
   - Every time Israel came with a lamb, goat, or other animal to the altar, it was a reminder that:
     - Sin is costly.  
     - A life is given so that life can be restored.  

3. **Hebrews 9:22 – without the shedding of blood, there is no forgiveness**  
   - Ruth quotes **Hebrews 9:22**:  
     - “Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins.”  
     - [Bible Gateway – Hebrews 9:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+9%3A22&version=NIV)  
   - This is not a call for endless third‑party sacrifices.  
   - It is pointing forward to the **one sacrifice** that God Himself will provide.  
   - The author is revealing that God Himself will **bear the cost** of reconciliation in the person of Jesus.

4. **The system was incomplete**  
   - The sacrificial system was **incomplete** and **insufficient**.  
   - It managed the **symptoms** of sin but did not solve the root problem.  
   - Sin kept returning; the relationship remained broken.  
   - The sacrifices were temporary, foreshadowing a final, once‑for‑all sacrifice.

### V. God’s answer in Jesus: blood sacrifice and the cross

1. **Romans 3:25–26 – the cross made sense**  
   - Ruth returns to **Romans 3:25–26**:  
     - God presented Jesus as the **sacrifice for sin**.  
     - People are made right with God when they believe that Jesus sacrificed His life, shedding His blood.  
     - This demonstrates God’s righteousness: He is just and the justifier of those who believe in Jesus.  
     - [Bible Gateway – Romans 3:25–26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+3%3A25-26&version=NIV)  
   - Ruth paraphrases verse 26 to include her name:  
     - “For He was looking ahead and including **Ruth** in what He would do in this present time.”  
   - This is a way of saying God designed the cross with **you** in mind, long before you came into the picture.

2. **Jesus’ blood: not appeasement, but loving commitment**  
   - Ruth stresses that the blood of Christ is **not** about:
     - Appeasing an angry God.  
     - Satisfying a God who demands blood every time we sin.  
   - It is about **God’s holy love going all the way**.  
   - Jesus absorbs the cost of our sin, once and for all, clearing the offense between us and God.  

3. **Eternal redemption obtained through Christ’s blood**  
   - Ruth references the idea that Jesus, as the **high priest**, entered the Most Holy Place by His own blood, obtaining **eternal redemption**.  
     - [Bible Gateway – Hebrews 9:11–12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+9%3A11-12&version=NIV)  
   - This redemption is not just for some distant future; it is effective for us **today**.  
   - It is both:
     - A **present reality** (we are forgiven and reconciled).  
     - A **future hope** (we will be made complete in His presence forever).  

4. **Jesus’ blood is poured into us**  
   - Ruth uses the image of a blood transfusion again:  
     - If blood carries life physically, then Jesus shedding His blood means He is **pouring His life into us**.  
   - This is:
     - **Re‑integrated life**: brokenness is healed.  
     - **Cleansed life**: guilt and shame are washed away.  
   - She underscores that God **sees** our mess, our brokenness, and our regret.  
     - He is not distant; He steps into it.  
     - The work is **finished**; we are not required to earn our way back.

5. **Adoption and freedom**  
   - Through Jesus’ sacrifice, God:
     - Sets us free from **bondage**.  
     - Heals what is **broken** within us.  
     - Restores us from **alienation**.  
     - **Adopts** us as His sons and daughters.  
   - Ruth joyfully summarizes:  
     - We get to live with the Father, not as orphans.  
     - We are not left to live in brokenness, but in family with God.  

6. **No more altar‑work needed**  
   - Ruth says our words, efforts, or “good works” added to the altar cannot change our standing with God.  
   - The work is already done.  
   - The blood sacrifice means that **Jesus’ life is for ours**—a one‑for‑one exchange.  
   - This is not a past theory; it is a living reality for our **day‑to‑day walk with Jesus** and our **future hope**.

### VI. Paul’s heart transplant: illustration of receiving a gift

1. **Paul’s failing heart**  
   - Ruth tells the story of her father‑in‑law, Paul, whose heart gradually failed over several years.  
   - Simple tasks like walking up stairs or getting into the car became nearly impossible.  
   - Doctors realized his heart was no longer pumping blood properly.  

2. **Surgeries and decline**  
   - After multiple failed surgeries, doctors concluded that Paul needed a **heart transplant**.  
   - The family had to sit with the reality of Paul’s decline and the uncertainty of transplant timelines.  

3. **The transplant list and the “yes”**  
   - Doctors suggested Paul be put on the transplant list; this meant:
     - More waiting.  
     - More uncertainty.  
     - The possibility of more disappointment.  
   - Ruth points out that it’s easy to say “yes” in hindsight, but it was hard in the moment.  
   - The family had already begun to accept that Paul’s decline was inevitable.  
   - Yet they chose to say **yes**.  

4. **The miracle of timing**  
   - Within **24 hours**, a match was found for Paul.  
   - In the middle of the night, her mother‑in‑law got a phone call about a heart.  
   - At first, she thought it was a prank; 24 hours is an incredibly short window for a transplant.  
   - The doctor said they had about **30 minutes** to accept.  
   - Once the family confirmed it was real, they said **yes** and drove to Stanford.  
   - Within hours, Paul had a new heart.  

5. **A donor’s unknown impact**  
   - Ruth shares that the donor:
     - Had no idea that one day Paul would walk at his son’s wedding.  
     - Would take afternoon bike rides with his wife.  
     - Would spend many nights around the dinner table with family and friends.  
   - The donor’s life became **life for Paul**.  
   - The gift of life only became effective when Paul and his family **received it**.  

6. **Application to the gospel**  
   - Ruth draws a direct parallel:  
     - A heart was offered, the surgery was ready, the operating room was prepared.  
     - But the gift of life only worked when Paul and his family **said “yes.”**  
   - So it is with Jesus:  
     - The cross is real.  
     - The sacrifice is complete.  
     - The “procedure” is ready.  
     - But the gift of life must be **received**.  
   - The cross is not a symbolic image; it is **actual life** for those who receive it.

### VII. The cross and our access to God

1. **The cross opens access**  
   - Ruth says the cross **opens access** to God.  
   - It tears down the barriers that once stood between us and God.  
   - The curtain in the temple was torn; we no longer need a priest to mediate continually.  

2. **Hebrews 10:19 – confidence through Jesus’ blood**  
   - Ruth quotes **Hebrews 10:19**:  
     - “Therefore, brothers and sisters, since we have confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus…”  
     - [Bible Gateway – Hebrews 10:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10%3A19&version=NIV)  
   - Our confidence is not in our ability to be perfect, fix ourselves, or perform for God.  
   - Our confidence is in **the blood of Jesus**, which grants us access.  

3. **You are worth the life of God**  
   - Ruth says emphatically:  
     - You are **worth the life of God**.  
   - God desires **communion** with you; He is not distant or indifferent.  
   - You do not need to:
     - Earn your way to God.  
     - Fix yourself first.  
     - Put together a perfect performance before you show up.  

4. **The life of Jesus is simply received**  
   - Ruth repeats that the life Jesus offers through His blood is to be **simply received**.  
   - It is not a complicated transaction; it is a surrendered acceptance.  
   - The invitation is to come as you are:  
     - In **doubt**.  
     - In **pain**.  
     - In **crisis**.  
   - The gift of life is for you right where you are.
